The short version

If you weigh small batches on a plate or a small bowl and don't mind a scale that turns off quickly, the budget Etekcity is enough for many kitchens. If you bake with wide mixing bowls, weigh onto sheet pans, or step away mid-recipe, the OXO Good Grips 11 lb scale with pull-out display solves those specific problems. It costs several times more, and it is not a precision scale.

What the OXO is for

Everyday cooking and baking by weight, portioning meat or produce, and weighing large or wide containers. The model in this guide is the OXO Good Grips 11 lb Stainless Steel Food Scale with Pull-Out Display, SKU 11214800 (Amazon listings also show part 11214800V1). Do not confuse it with the older 1130800 listing, which Amazon has marked as having a newer version available.

Verified features (manufacturer, OXO)

  • 11 lb / 5 kg capacity, 1/8-oz (0.1 oz) and 1-g increments
  • Display pulls away from the base so a wide bowl does not shade it, and slides back in for storage
  • Zero (tare) and lb/kg unit buttons, optional backlight, and a meter showing how much capacity remains
  • Removable stainless platform, hand-wash only (the platform and base are not dishwasher-safe)
  • Four AAA batteries included

What independent testers found

  • Serious Eats named it its best kitchen scale after testing 24 models. Their reasons: the pull-out display, sealed push buttons, a removable tray, and a standby mode that stayed on about 5 minutes and then held the last reading about 10 more minutes. Their downsides: it was off by one gram more often than the other winners (mostly with a 5-gram weight), it reads only to 0.1 oz, and flour can collect under the platform and around the display.
  • America's Test Kitchen also picks it as its winner, calling it "accurate within a couple grams" for cooking and baking. ATK says it is not precise enough for weighing coffee, and found the area under the pulled-out display harder to clean.
  • Wirecutter disagrees. It tested "the new version" of this scale and found it "consistently inaccurate, especially with smaller measurements."
  • How the budget alternative compares: in Serious Eats' testing, the Etekcity EK6015 was dropped for readings off by up to 3 grams and was sometimes slow to show changes. Etekcity's listing gives a 2-minute auto-off and a 2-gram minimum measurement. See VoltPick's Etekcity kitchen scale guide for the budget option already on this site.

Pros (supported by the sources above)

  • Easier to read under big bowls and pans
  • Longer standby window, so you're less likely to lose a reading mid-recipe
  • Removable, washable platform and sealed buttons
  • 11 lb capacity handles large batches

Limitations and reasons not to buy

  • Costs much more than basic scales like the Etekcity
  • Not for coffee or very small amounts. Testers saw 1–2 g swings at small weights, and Wirecutter rated the newer version inaccurate.
  • 0.1-oz resolution in US units
  • Needs batteries (4×AAA), and crumbs can collect under the platform and display tether
  • Hand-wash only

Who it suits

Home bakers and batch cooks who weigh into large bowls, and anyone frustrated by fast auto-off.

Who should skip it

Coffee brewers or anyone weighing to a fraction of a gram (look at a precision scale instead), and occasional users who are fine with a basic scale.
