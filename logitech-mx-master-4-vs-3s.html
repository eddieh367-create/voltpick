Logitech MX Master 4 vs MX Master 3S: Which Should You Buy?

Both share the same core tracking specs. The choice comes down to weight, haptics, the extra button, and what comes in the box.

What stays the same (Logitech specs)

200–8,000 DPI Darkfield sensor that tracks on glass (4 mm minimum thickness), MagSpeed scroll wheel and thumb wheel, Bluetooth LE plus Logi Bolt support, up to about 70 days per charge, and 3 hours of use from a 1-minute charge. Both report at 125 Hz per Logitech's MX Master 4 spec sheet and Neowin's review. Customization requires the Logi Options+ app on Windows or macOS.

The MX Master 4 is mouse M/N MR0118; the Amazon Graphite listing used here is model 910-007558. The MX Master 3S is mouse M/N MR0077. It comes in two editions: the Standard Edition includes a USB-A Logi Bolt receiver and a charging cable, and the Bluetooth Edition is the mouse and documentation only. What reviewers found

  • Neowin (bought the 4 to replace a faulty 3S) praised the much-improved materials, which shouldn't turn sticky the way the 3S coating did, plus the screws you can reach for repairs and the polished software. Their criticisms: the 4 is still heavy, still 125 Hz, has no onboard memory (so custom buttons need Options+, which can be a problem on locked-down work PCs), the USB-C port charges only, and the price premium is mostly for materials and haptics. They also found the haptics useful mainly with the Actions Ring and a few apps, such as Photoshop, Lightroom and DaVinci Resolve.
  • TechRadar's writer, who owns both, still calls the 3S the better buy. They point to its lower price, and say the 4's extra weight and size make the 3S better for smaller hands and long workdays.
  • A personal side-by-side (Daniel Schroeder's blog) liked the 4's extra button but returned it. The reasons were the extra weight and accidental presses on the thumb panel, which is more slippery than the 3S's rubberized grip.

Choose the MX Master 3S if

You have smaller hands or notice weight over a long day, you want the lower price, or Bluetooth-only use is fine. Note that the Bluetooth Edition includes no receiver or cable; a Logi Bolt receiver is sold separately.

Choose the MX Master 4 if

You want haptics or the Actions Ring in supported apps, an extra programmable button, a USB-C receiver in the box for desktops without good Bluetooth, or a finish that reviewers expect to wear better.

Reasons to skip both

You need a high polling rate (for gaming), settings stored on the mouse for computers where you can't install software, a left-handed or small or travel mouse, or a wired mode.

Before you buy

Check the edition and box contents (3S Standard vs Bluetooth Edition), check your computer's USB ports (the 3S Standard receiver is USB-A; the 4's dongle is USB-C), and make sure you have a USB-C cable for charging.
